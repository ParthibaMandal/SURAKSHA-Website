## 1. Upstream Catchment Hydrology

The Ganges river originates at the confluence of the **Bhagirathi** (emerging from Gangotri Glacier at Gaumukh, 3,920m MSL) and the **Alaknanda** (originating from Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak glaciers) at Devprayag.

During pre-monsoon and early monsoon (May–July), snow and glacial melt contribute $25\%–40\%$ of upper river baseflow. Sudden warm atmospheric river events accelerate cryosphere ablation, creating antecedent channel saturation downstream before the primary monsoon rains strike Bihar.

## 2. Flood Wave Propagation: Devprayag to Patna

Hydraulic routing down the 1,000+ kilometer Ganga corridor exhibits well-defined wave celerity:

| Reach | River Distance | Channel Gradient | Flood Wave Travel Time ($T_{\text{travel}}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gangotri → Rishikesh** | ~240 km | $1:80$ (Steep bedrock gorge) | 6–10 hours |
| **Rishikesh → Haridwar** | ~30 km | $1:400$ (Bouldery piedmont) | 2–3 hours |
| **Haridwar → Kanpur** | ~480 km | $1:4,000$ (Alluvial plain) | 24–36 hours |
| **Kanpur → Prayagraj (Allahabad)**| ~220 km | $1:8,000$ (Braided alluvial) | 16–22 hours |
| **Prayagraj → Varanasi** | ~140 km | $1:10,000$ (Low gradient) | 12–16 hours |
| **Varanasi → Patna (Gandhi Ghat)**| ~240 km | $1:12,000$ (Extremely flat plain)| **24–30 hours** |

> [!NOTE]
> A major hydrometeorological or glacial pulse in the upper Uttarakhand Himalaya requires approximately **72 to 96 hours (3–4 days)** to propagate to Gandhi Ghat, Patna, allowing significant forecast lead time if upstream telemetries are integrated.
